An unexplained observation is a reason to investigate. It tells us that the available information has not produced an adequate identification. It does not, by itself, establish an unusual vehicle, a foreign operation, or a failure of known physics.

That distinction makes unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) relevant to security work. The handling of a report affects airspace awareness, safety, and public trust. The methods used to collect evidence and communicate uncertainty are familiar problems for anyone who investigates incidents.

This article reviews selected official sources available in the context of the December 2025 post. It is not a census of every reported sighting or a new analysis of raw sensor data.

## Start with the observation

“UAP” describes an unresolved observation within the reporting system using the term. Definitions and scope differ among agencies. The label should preserve uncertainty rather than imply a particular explanation.

A comparison with security operations is helpful. An untriaged alert may reflect a threat, ordinary activity, or a collection problem. Its severity must be assessed from the context; being unclassified does not automatically make it high severity.

For an aerial report, relevant information might include the observer's location, direction of view, timestamps, sensor settings, weather, and the original recording. Without distance and viewing geometry, apparent motion can be difficult to interpret.

## Read U.S. investigations within their remit

### AARO's historical review

The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office's [Historical Record Report, Volume I](https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf), published in March 2024, reviews U.S. government investigations and related claims. AARO reports that it found no evidence that the reviewed investigations confirmed extraterrestrial technology, and no verifiable support for the alleged possession or reverse engineering of such technology.

That is a finding of the review, with a defined source base and scope. It should be attributed to AARO. An unresolved case does not reverse the burden of proof, but a review's conclusion should remain open to examination through its methods and evidence.

Historical work and ongoing case resolution also serve different functions. One evaluates the record of programs and allegations; the other examines particular reports for identification and potential safety or security significance.

### NASA's independent study

NASA's [2023 independent study report](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf) asks how scientific methods and unclassified data could support the study of UAP. It emphasizes limitations in data quality, calibration, and contextual information.

This was a methodological study, rather than a comprehensive investigation of every sighting. Its practical contribution is to explain why observations need consistent collection and metadata before researchers can make reliable comparisons.

A model can help identify patterns, but it cannot recover an unrecorded sensor setting by guessing confidently. Preserve the distinction between measured information and a reconstruction.

## Compare international approaches

### France: classify the outcome of the investigation

France's GEIPAN, within the CNES space agency, publishes case material and an explicit [classification method](https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/node/25). Its categories distinguish identified and probably identified reports, reports lacking sufficient information, and cases remaining unexplained after investigation.

That separation is useful. “Insufficient information” and “unexplained after analysis” describe different conditions. Combining them in a single mystery count would conceal the quality of the evidence.

Percentages also need a date, denominator, and explanation of which cases are included. The classification method is more useful here than an unsourced claim about what proportion of all sightings has been resolved.

### Canada: examine the reporting process

The [Sky Canada Project](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project) examines how reports are collected and handled. Its governance focus directs attention to reporting channels, data sharing, and the public's ability to understand the process.

This matters because a report cannot be investigated effectively if people do not know where to send it or if related information remains in separate systems. A reporting arrangement should make clear what happens after submission and what kind of response is possible.

### Brazil: make records available for scrutiny

Brazil's National Archives provides public access to historical UFO-related material and describes the [May 19, 1986 reporting context](https://www.gov.br/arquivonacional/pt-br/canais_atendimento/imprensa/noticias/serie-relatos-extraterrestres/). Archival access allows readers to examine what observers and officials recorded.

An official record can establish that an observation was reported and how it was handled. It does not automatically verify every interpretation in the document. Dates, provenance, original wording, and later analysis should remain distinct.

## Assess the physical security question

Some reports may concern ordinary aircraft, balloons, drones, astronomical objects, or sensor effects. Others may require further investigation for flight safety or surveillance concerns. The task is to establish the explanation supported by the case.

Where a report concerns a sensitive site, combine the observation with relevant operational records. Ask whether authorized activity, weather, or a known platform fits the time and location. Keep the possibility of a collection fault in view.

A foreign intelligence explanation requires evidence of more than an unidentified object. Conversely, uncertainty about origin should not prevent a proportionate response to an immediate safety concern.

## Assess the information environment

A real recording can acquire a misleading caption. A synthetic image can be circulated as an observation. A partial disclosure can be interpreted more broadly than the underlying document supports.

Those mechanisms create opportunities for manipulation, but an opportunity is not evidence that an operation occurred. Trace the source and distribution of a claim, identify the alteration or deception, and examine any proposed attribution separately.

Secrecy can also make public evaluation difficult. That does not establish that a particular secret is being concealed. It does create a reason for agencies to explain their methods and limitations wherever they can.

## Build a reporting process that preserves evidence

A useful intake process specifies the minimum information needed and preserves original files. It records missing data explicitly and provides a way to add new observations without overwriting the earlier account.

Triage should distinguish a potential safety issue from a routine identification task. Analysis should record the hypotheses tested, the evidence for each, and why the case remains open or was closed.

People should be able to report an observation without being expected to supply an explanation. Reducing stigma can help collection, while clear standards keep the investigation tied to evidence.

## Apply the lessons to hybrid defense

Cyber, physical, and public-information records may describe different parts of an incident. Coordinating those records can reveal a relationship, but analysts also need to test whether the events are independent.

A drone sighting, a suspicious network connection, and public rumors might share an origin. They might also coincide. Record the evidence linking them before treating them as one campaign.

The lesson is practical: establish the observation, preserve its context, test explanations, and communicate the result with its limits. That gives unresolved reports a useful place in security work while leaving room for new evidence.
